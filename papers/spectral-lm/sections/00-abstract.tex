\begin{abstract}
A language model's hidden state could be stored as a few Fourier coefficients
of a longer virtual signal and processed by per-frequency operators, which
would cut parameters and arithmetic. We first show that a basis change alone
cannot do this: a decoder whose maps on the residual stream are dense absorbs
any orthogonal basis into its weights, and truncating its spectrum yields an
ordinary narrow decoder. We then define SpectralLM, a decoder whose residual
stream is the lowest $m$ coefficients of a real Fourier basis and whose
feed-forward networks are replaced by a spectral mixer with $3Cm$ parameters:
per-mode filters around a gated product taken in the signal domain, which
couples frequencies through spectral convolution. We implement it in an
existing training and inference engine with gradient-checked operators and a
fast-transform path gated against a dense reference, and register a
comparison on TinyStories against width-matched conventional decoders and
against Qwen3-0.6B in bits per byte. \StatusAbstract
\end{abstract}
