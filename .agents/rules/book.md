# Book rules

Read this before you write or change anything under `docs/book/` or
`scripts/book/`.

`docs/book/` is a textbook, *From First Principles to Intelligent Machines*:
machine learning taught from arithmetic to a complete engine, with brain's
implementation and history as the case study. It is published with the docs
(its own part of `docs/manifest.yaml`) and typeset as its own PDF
(`make book/pdf`). It is not product documentation: it may explain internals,
history and measurements that `docs/` must not, under the rules below.
Status (which chapters are written, verified, open) lives in
`.agents/roadmap/book.md`, never in the book.

## The specification

`docs/book/book.yaml` lists every volume and chapter in reading order. Each
chapter declares the question it answers, the concepts it **introduces**, the
concepts it **requires** and the brain crates it explains. `make check/book`
enforces that a concept is introduced once, that nothing is required before
the chapter that introduces it, and that every crate has a chapter. A chapter
file is `docs/book/<volume dir>/<NN>-<id>.md`; it exists once written, and the
navigation (`make generate/book/navigation`) lists only written chapters.

Changing the order or the concept lists is a change to the curriculum: keep
the graph valid, and when a chapter needs an idea nobody introduces, add it to
the chapter where a reader should first meet it.

## Writing a chapter

- **Teach without brain.** Every concept must be understandable and
  implementable from the text alone, in any language. brain is evidence that
  the idea works, never the definition of it. Begin with the problem, never
  with a source file.
- **Write it, do not fill it in.** A chapter reads like a thoughtful author
  explaining something they care about, not like a form. The first heading is
  `# <title from book.yaml>`, followed by the question as an italic line; the
  body after that is the author's own. Open with a situation, a puzzle, a
  failure or a number, whatever makes this subject urgent, and let the reason
  for each idea come out of the story of the previous one. Headings name the
  subject of their section ("Why a column walk misses the cache", not "Common
  mistakes" or "What this is for"), and no two chapters should feel cut from
  the same template. `make check/book` rejects template-slot labels and any
  heading or run-in label recycled across chapters.
- **The end of a chapter is reference apparatus**, the one place every
  chapter is alike so a reader can find it: `## Exercises`, `## Key terms`
  and `## Answers`, optionally preceded by `## Check your understanding`, a
  `## Summary` and `## References`. A project chapter (`project-*`) has
  `## Milestones` and `## Acceptance tests`, and optionally `## Extensions`.
- **Prerequisites are recalled, not listed.** Early in the chapter, before the
  new material leans on them, the reader is asked to bring back 3 to 6 ideas
  from the chapter's `requires` (at least one from three or more chapters
  back, when the chapter has such prerequisites), each naming where it was
  taught, as questions woven into the opening or set apart briefly in
  whatever form suits the chapter. Their answers go in `## Answers`.
- **Check your understanding** has 4 to 8 questions that ask the reader to
  explain, predict an outcome, or find the error in a short argument or
  computation. Answers go in `## Answers`.
- **Terms.** Bold a term where it is defined, never use it before, and give
  its everyday meaning when that differs from the technical one. `## Key
  terms` lists exactly the concepts in the chapter's `introduces`, one line
  each: `- **term**: plain-language definition. <!-- concept: <id> -->`. A
  definition never relies on a term the reader has not met. The glossary
  (`make generate/book/glossary`) is built from these lines.
- **Purpose throughout, in the prose.** Every idea arrives because the
  reader needs it; say why in the sentence that introduces it, never under a
  label. The summary says why the next chapter follows.
- **No negative-space sections.** Never write "what this is not" or
  "non-goals" sections; state scope positively in a sentence where needed.
- **Projects** end each volume: a larger build in the reader's own language
  using the whole volume, split into milestones that each end in a check the
  reader can run, with acceptance tests stated as concrete inputs and
  expected outputs (generated by a worked module) and optional extensions.
- **Derive, do not assert.** Every equation is built from concepts already
  introduced (by this chapter or a required one), every symbol is defined, and
  intermediate steps are shown. Use the symbols in `docs/book/notation.md`.
- **Algorithms in the book's pseudocode** (`docs/book/pseudocode.md`): typed,
  0-indexed, row-major, explicit broadcasts, explicit randomness, `require`
  for every input assumption. Never Rust, Python or a framework call.
- **Numbers are computed, never typed.** A worked example is a block
  `<!-- worked: <module>.<function> -->` ... `<!-- /worked -->` whose content
  is generated by `scripts/book/worked/<module>.py`: plain Python, standard
  library only, randomness only from `bookrng.stream(<name>)` so that no
  module carries a seed literal, written from the chapter's own description (it is the second,
  independent implementation). `make generate/book/worked` fills it in.
- **Every worked example is reproducible from the page.** It prints, or
  names exactly, all of its inputs: data, constants, starting values, step
  sizes and loop bounds. Random inputs come from `bookrng.generator(<name>)`
  (the book's own SplitMix64, as its randomness chapter specifies), and the
  page states the seed (`bookrng.seed_of(<name>)`) and the order in which the
  values are drawn; before that chapter, also print the drawn values when
  they are few. Each table states its tolerance or prints enough digits to
  compare against, and every number quoted in prose matches the table it
  comes from.
- **Only stable numbers get full digits.** Every run in a multi-run table
  prints its seed. A run that diverges or is chaotic (a blow-up, a limit
  cycle, NaN) is printed as its qualitative outcome or to its stable digits,
  never to full precision another implementation cannot match. A number
  that depends on a library function names it exactly.
- **Pseudocode uses only what `docs/book/pseudocode.md` defines.** A chapter
  that needs a new operation (`sign`, `sort`, bit shifts, complex arithmetic)
  defines it there in the same change, including edge cases such as
  `sign(0)`. Every symbol is in `docs/book/notation.md`, and no symbol or
  word carries two meanings without the text saying so.
- **Figures are generated SVG.** Block diagrams are placed on an exact grid
  with `scripts/book/svgdiagram.py` (aligned columns, equal panels, labels
  clear of the lines); Graphviz is for trees and graphs that need automatic
  layout. Other structure diagrams are Graphviz sources
  (`scripts/book/figures/<volume dir>/<name>.dot`); anything that plots numbers
  is a Python `render()` using `scripts/book/svgplot.py` and computing its data
  with the chapter's arithmetic. `make generate/book/figures` writes
  `docs/book/<volume dir>/img/<name>.svg`; embed it as `![alt](img/<name>.svg)`
  with alt text that states what the figure shows. Keep each SVG small (the
  gate's budget is 120 KiB; aim far lower); no raster images, no remote
  images, nothing copied from papers or the web. Render to PNG
  (`scripts/book/figures.py --png scratchpad/book/png`) and look at it before
  committing: a figure that is wrong is worse than none.
- **Sources are claims, not facts.** Commit messages and knowledge entries
  record what someone believed at the time; some were later corrected, some
  overstate, and their numbers belong to one setup on one day. Use them to
  find the story, then confirm what the book states against the code, the
  tests or the diff. Teach the general lesson and the mechanism; leave out
  incidental detail (exact timings, ticket-like specifics) the reader does
  not need. When a claim cannot be confirmed, leave it out.
- **History is cited, not narrated from memory.** A historical claim cites
  `commit <hash>` (at least 9 hex digits, verified to exist) or
  `knowledge #N`. Mine the evidence with `scripts/book/history.py extract`
  (writes `scratchpad/book/history.jsonl`) and read the commit itself before
  citing it. Describe what the commit shows, not what it was hoped to show.
- **Speed is stated as work, not time.** The hardware-independent cost of a
  computation is the arithmetic it needs (FLOPs) and the data it must move
  (bytes); an improvement is fewer of either, or a higher achieved fraction
  of the device's own roofline. Seconds depend on the machine and are only
  ever a dated observation of a stated setup. Derive or count the FLOPs and
  bytes of an algorithm before quoting any time for it.
- **Measurements are history.** A number about speed or throughput is stated
  as a dated observation of a specific setup ("in July 2026, on one GPU, ...")
  and carries the perf-number gate's reviewed-exception comment
  (`<!-- perf-number: historical measurement, commit <hash> -->`) on the line
  before. Never present one as what the reader's hardware will do.
- **Scope and naming.** The repository naming rule applies: name no project
  brain does not depend on, describe callers generically. Commit messages
  sometimes name them; a chapter citing such a commit does not repeat the name.
- **Paths and links.** Brain paths are written as `crates/<crate>/...` in
  backticks and must exist. Link other chapters by relative path. No
  machine-specific absolute paths. No U+2014 em dash.
- **External sources.** Cite papers by authors, title and year in a chapter's
  references section. Do not reproduce figures, tables or long passages from
  them.
- **Exercises** are tagged **[Calculate]**, **[Implement]** or
  **[Investigate]**. Every [Implement] exercise names the worked example the
  reader's implementation must reproduce.

## Verifying a chapter

A chapter is done when `make check/book check/generated/book docs/check` pass,
`make book/pdf` typesets it without errors or overflow, its figures have been
looked at, and an independent reader (a fresh agent given only the book, not
the repository) has implemented its [Implement] exercises in a language other
than Rust from the text alone and reproduced the worked examples. Record the
outcome in the ledger. Each finished volume gets such a reader pass across
all its chapters and its project; the volume is done when every finding is
fixed or recorded as open in the ledger.
